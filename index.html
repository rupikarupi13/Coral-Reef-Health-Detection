🧬 Problem Statement & Research Context

Coral reefs cover less than 1% of the ocean floor yet support over 25% of all marine species. They protect coastlines, support fisheries feeding over 1 billion people, and contribute an estimated $375 billion annually to global economies through tourism, fisheries and coastal protection. Today they face an existential crisis driven by climate change, ocean acidification and human pressure.

🌡️

Thermal Bleaching

When sea surface temperatures rise 1–2°C above seasonal maxima for 3–4 weeks, corals expel their zooxanthellae algae and lose 90% of their energy source. Prolonged heat causes irreversible mass mortality within weeks.

🔬

Ocean Acidification

Oceans absorb 30% of CO₂ emissions. Ocean pH has dropped from 8.2 to 8.1 since industrialisation. A projected pH of 7.95 by 2100 will dissolve coral skeletons and prevent new reef growth entirely.

💧

Turbidity & Pollution

Agricultural runoff and coastal development block sunlight critical for coral photosynthesis. Nutrient loading triggers algal blooms that outcompete and suffocate coral colonies permanently.

⏱️

Detection Latency

Traditional reef monitoring relies on diver surveys conducted quarterly or annually — far too slow. Bleaching events can kill entire reefs within weeks. By the time bleaching is confirmed, intervention windows are already closed.

📚 Coral Reef Science & Conservation
🌡️

Thermal Bleaching Mechanism

At 1–2°C above seasonal maxima, corals expel zooxanthellae algae providing up to 90% of their energy. Bleaching does not immediately kill corals — mortality becomes likely if temperatures persist beyond 4 weeks without recovery.

🔬

Ocean Acidification

Oceans absorb ~30% of anthropogenic CO₂, forming carbonic acid. Current ocean pH is ~8.1 vs pre-industrial 8.2. Aragonite saturation required for coral skeletal growth drops dramatically below pH 8.0, weakening and dissolving reef structures.

💧

Turbidity & Light Attenuation

High turbidity (NTU >10) reduces sunlight for photosynthesis. Runoff from agriculture and construction introduces suspended sediments and nutrients causing smothering, algal overgrowth and coral suffocation.

🧂

Salinity Stress

Optimal salinity for corals is 32–42 ppt. Freshwater intrusion from heavy rainfall causes osmotic stress. Evaporation in enclosed lagoons creates hypersaline conditions. Both extremes cause physiological stress and bleaching events.

🫧

Dissolved Oxygen

Reef health requires 6–8 mg/L DO. Eutrophication from nutrient runoff fuels algal blooms. Bacterial decomposition depletes oxygen, creating hypoxic zones (DO <2 mg/L). Fish kills and coral mortality can occur within hours.

🛰️

Satellite Remote Sensing

NOAA CoralTemp provides global daily SST at 5km resolution. ISRO Oceansat-3 carries Ocean Color Monitor and SST Monitor. These feeds detect thermal anomalies (Degree Heating Weeks) predicting bleaching 2–4 weeks in advance.

🇮🇳

India's Marine Biodiversity

India's ~2,375 km² of coral reefs span four regions: Lakshadweep (fringing and platform reefs), Andaman & Nicobar (fringing, barrier and patch reefs), Gulf of Mannar (patch reefs) and Gulf of Kutch. They support ~1,192 fish species.

📉

Global Crisis Data

The 2022 GCRMN report confirmed 14% of reefs destroyed since 2009. 50% of all reef coral lost since 1950. Mass bleaching events are now 5× more frequent than the 1980s. Without climate action, 70–90% of reefs will be lost by 2050.

💡

Conservation Technologies

Active interventions include coral gardening (nursery-grown fragments transplanted to degraded reefs), assisted gene flow (heat-resistant genotypes), 3D-printed reef structures, and cloud seeding to reduce SST. AI early warning is now critical for rapid coordinated response.

ℹ️ About ReefGuard AI

ReefGuard AI is a prototype for automated coral reef health monitoring and bleaching detection. It processes 8 real-time environmental parameters through a multi-factor AI risk scoring engine to classify reef health and predict bleaching risk before visual symptoms appear.

The platform combines live satellite sea surface temperature feeds, an interactive global reef map with 15+ monitored zones, per-researcher session history, trend charts and a reef science education module.

Built to address the critical need for scalable, affordable, real-time coral reef monitoring — enabling marine biologists, conservationists and policymakers to act within narrow intervention windows before bleaching becomes irreversible.
